## 1. Hook Mechanics (First 1-3 Seconds)

### The 3-Second Window
- 65% of viewers who watch the first 3 seconds will watch for 10+ seconds
- 45% will watch for 30+ seconds
- Shorts with an immediate hook in the first 2 seconds retain 19% more viewers

### Hook Types That Work

**Curiosity Gap:** Open a loop, tease a payoff, delay the explanation. "I tracked every dollar I spent for 30 days" (specific + result hidden). Weak: "I tried something." Strong: specific detail + missing outcome.

**Pattern Interrupt:** Unexpected visual/sound/statement that breaks scrolling autopilot. The brain stops when something violates expected patterns.

**Visual Density:** Minimum 3 different shots or visual elements in the first 3 seconds (B-roll cut, text overlay animation, quick zoom).

**Multi-Component Hook:** Visual + text + verbal reinforcing the same message simultaneously. A spoken hook combined with an on-screen graphic drives significantly higher engagement than either alone.

### What Kills Hooks
- "Hey guys!", "What's up everyone!", "In today's video..." — zero information, zero reason to stop
- Slow builds without immediate visual stimulus
- Generic openings without specificity

### For Faceless/Satisfying Content Specifically
- Immediate visual stimulus (paint pouring, color transformation, object being cut)
- Text overlay with curiosity trigger ("Watch what happens when...")
- Unexpected color/shape result teased visually before reveal
- No intro needed — start mid-action

## 2. Retention Mechanics

### Completion Rate Benchmarks

**TikTok (2026 data):**

| Retention Rate | Level | Algorithm Response |
|---|---|---|
| Under 40% | Poor | Won't distribute |
| 40-60% | Average | Limited viral potential |
| 60-70% | Good | Tests with larger audiences |
| 70-80% | Great | Viral potential unlocked |
| 80%+ | Excellent | Aggressive promotion |
| 90%+ | Rare | Mastered retention |

**YouTube Shorts:**
- 70% completion rate = 30% more impressions
- Top-performing Shorts hit 80-90% average completion rates
- Viral Shorts often have >100% APV (Average Percentage Viewed) = viewers watching multiple times

**Cross-platform:**
- Videos under 30 seconds: 28% higher completion rates vs longer formats
- Videos >60 seconds: sharply declining engagement

### The 70% Rule (TikTok)
- 70%+ completion rate on first 200 views determines whether video graduates to larger distribution
- Up from 50% threshold in 2024
- Videos below 70% completion rarely exceed 10,000 views

### Loop Techniques
- Loop-friendly videos get 65% more rewatches
- Seamless loops connect end to start, making restart unnoticeable
- Find a "rest state" — point where subject and background are at calmest/most repetitive
- Optimal loop source: 3-10 seconds long
- Audio must be seamless too — jarring audio cut ruins the illusion instantly

### Delayed Payoff / Progress Bar Effect
- Tease the result early but delay full reveal
- "Open a loop → tease a payoff → delay the explanation"
- For satisfying content: show the beginning state, hint at transformation, make them watch to see the result

### Duration Sweet Spots (Data-Backed)

| Content Type | Optimal Duration | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Entertainment/comedy | 18-25 seconds | Quick payoff |
| Educational | 35-45 seconds | Needs clear payoff |
| Tutorial | 25-40 seconds | Step completion |
| Maximum virality | 11-18 seconds | Highest completion rates |
| Engagement sweet spot | 15-30 seconds | Retention often >80% |
| General safe range | 20-45 seconds | Story + no fatigue |

YouTube allows up to 3 minutes (since 2024), but most successful Shorts live in 15-60 second range.

## 3. Algorithm Mechanics

### YouTube Shorts Algorithm Signals (Ranked)

1. **Completion rate / Average Percentage Viewed** — strongest signal
2. **Rewatch behavior** — replays signal exceptional value
3. **Early swipe-through patterns** — swipe-away = death signal
4. **Engagement velocity** — speed of likes/comments/shares after publish
5. **Shares** — strongest engagement signal for broader distribution
6. **Saves** — signals long-term value
7. **Search value** — content that gets searched for

**NOT a ranking factor:** CTR is irrelevant for Shorts (autoplay is default in Shorts feed, YouTube doesn't track CTR there).

### Algorithm Testing Model (YouTube)
1. Short shown to small initial test group
2. Algorithm watches: completion, replay, swipe-away rate
3. High completion + replay → expanded to larger audiences
4. Quick swipe-away → stops being pushed
5. Initial test range: 1k-10k views

### TikTok Algorithm Differences
- Completion rate is #1 signal (same as YouTube)
- Total watch time now more important than just 3-second retention
- Rewatch rate: 15-20%+ is excellent
- Distribution starts with 100-200 followers, expands to 1,000-5,000 if retention holds
- Loop-friendly content gets preferential treatment

### Key Thresholds
- **70% completion** → viral potential unlocked (both platforms)
- **80%+ completion** → aggressive promotion
- **100%+ APV** → viewers rewatching = exponential reach
- **3% share-to-view ratio** → "punching well above weight in virality potential"
- **8-10% combined interaction rate** → strong algorithm signal

### Multi-Platform Signal
YouTube Shorts algorithm now rewards content shared across multiple platforms.

## 4. Engagement Mechanics

### CTA Patterns That Drive Comments
- Move CTAs INTO the video, not captions — viewers are already invested
- Mid-roll prompts: "Tag someone who'd relate"
- End-frame questions: "Which would you choose?"
- Spoken CTA + on-screen graphic = highest conversion
- Framework: Hook → Value → CTA

### Share Triggers
- Shares are the real currency — a share means someone found it valuable enough to put in front of their network
- Shorts with above-average share counts reach exponentially wider audiences
- Target: 3%+ share-to-view ratio
- What drives shares: "I need to show this to someone" moment — surprise, beauty, usefulness

### What Makes People Rewatch
- Seamless loops (can't tell where video starts/ends)
- "Did I see that right?" moments — unexpected transformations
- Satisfying content with subtle details noticed on second watch
- Puzzle/challenge content where viewer tries to solve it
- High information density that can't be absorbed in one viewing

### Engagement Benchmarks
- Short-form videos: 2.5x more engagement than long-form
- Videos under 1 minute: ~50% engagement rate
- Average short-form engagement: 4-8% depending on platform

## 5. Format Patterns (Satisfying/Puzzle Niche)

### Proven Oddly Satisfying Categories
1. **Soap cutting/slicing** — crunchy sound + visual precision, millions of views
2. **Slime mixing/stretching** — ASMR-friendly, strong on TikTok
3. **Paint mixing** — psychedelic swirls, no commentary needed (@tonesterpaints: 1.4M followers, @paintturner: 4M followers)
4. **Craft/artwork process** — pottery, resin art, fastest-growing category in 2025
5. **Perfectly looping animations** — Andreas Wannerstedt: 663K followers, millions of views per video
6. **Kinetic sand cutting** — visual + audio satisfaction
7. **Rust removal / restoration** — transformation arc

### Paint Mixing Specific Patterns
- Minimal style works: no commentary, no soundtrack, just the process
- Industrial/mechanical context adds "How It's Made" appeal
- Reaching into frame = ASMR trigger
- Taking requests from commenters drives engagement
- Color reveal at the end = built-in payoff

### "Guess the..." Format Analysis
- "Can you spot it?" challenges attract millions of viewers
- Color difference tests (spot the different shade) — featured on Reddit with 1M+ views
- Competition + curiosity + instant gratification = perfect viral formula
- Viewers share results and challenge friends → organic reach multiplier
- Works because it's short, interactive, and shareable

### What Works for AI-Generated Satisfying Content
- Synchronized visuals + music rhythm (tempo, energy matching)
- Smooth movements are critical — jerky AI artifacts break the satisfying feeling
- Extreme close-ups work well (hides AI imperfections)
- Leading channels: Oddly Satisfying (4.41M subs, 2.27B total views as of Nov 2025)

## 6. Technical Parameters

### Optimal Duration
- **Target: 15-30 seconds** for satisfying/visual content (highest completion rates)
- Maximum virality zone: 11-18 seconds
- Never exceed 60 seconds unless content demands it (completion drops sharply)

### Text Placement Safe Zones (1080x1920 px)

| Zone | Margin | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Top | 288-380 px | Title + channel info overlay |
| Bottom | 672 px (or 380 px from bottom) | Comments, likes, subscribe button |
| Left | 48-60 px | UI elements |
| Right | 120-192 px | Action buttons |
| **Safe zone** | ~960 x 1160 px centered | Guaranteed visible |

**Rules:**
- Text centered or slightly above center for maximum readability
- Captions: at least 120 px from top, 300 px from bottom
- CTA text: 350 px above bottom to avoid UI hiding

### Music Volume Ratios
- Background music: 10 dB below vocals/main audio
- During speech: drop music to 20-30% volume
- Visual-only moments: bring music back up
- Never exceed 0 dB (distortion)
- For faceless satisfying content with no speech: music can be primary but keep it under -3 dB peak

### Aspect Ratio
- 9:16 vertical (1080 x 1920 px) — mandatory for all platforms
- Content composed within safe zones

## 7. Title/Description/Tags

### Title Formulas (Shorts-Specific)
- **CTR is irrelevant in Shorts feed** (autoplay, no click needed)
- Titles matter for: search discovery, Shorts shelf categorization, context
- Main keyword in first 3-5 words
- Keep under 55-60 characters
- Hashtags in title: +14% more views [unverified]

### Effective Title Patterns
- Curiosity: "Why your latest Short flopped..."
- Specificity + brackets: "Beginner Guide to YouTube Shorts [2025 Update]"
- Emotional triggers + short sentences
- "You" and "we" to pull viewers in

### Hashtag Strategy
- Use 3-5 highly relevant hashtags (max 8, never exceed 60 total — YouTube ignores all)
- Always include #Shorts for categorization
- Mix: 1-2 broad (#Satisfying) + 1-2 niche (#PaintMixing) + 1 branded if applicable
- No hashtag stuffing — algorithm penalizes it

### Description
- First 125 characters appear in preview — include primary keyword naturally
- Briefly describe content with searchable keywords
- Context beyond the title
- Hashtags at the end

## 8. Posting Strategy

### Frequency
- Sweet spot: 3-7 Shorts per week
- More uploads = more chances for a hit, but quality > quantity
- Channels with 200+ Shorts posted show more consistent view growth

### Consistency
- Regular schedule trains audience expectations (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri at 7 PM)
- Consistency matters more than specific timing
- YouTube: "no magic 'post at 5pm for maximum reach' rule"
- Timing is NOT a direct ranking factor — content performance is

### Growth Timelines
- Initial test: 1k-10k views per Short
- Consistent posting + high retention → gradual audience building
- "The Flattening" (late 2025): Shorts older than ~28-30 days get deprioritized — consistent new content more critical than ever
- Viral breakout: unpredictable, but probability increases with volume + quality

### Posting Strategy for Our Case (60 videos/month target)
- ~2 videos/day = above average frequency
- Critical: each video must feel distinct (not templated/repetitive) — see AI Content Policy
- Batch production + scheduled posting to maintain consistency

## 9. AI Content Policy

### YouTube Policy (Effective July 15, 2025)
- "Repetitious content" renamed to **"inauthentic content"**
- Definition: mass-produced or repetitive content, including content created using templates with minimal variation

### What's Allowed
- AI as a **tool** to enhance storytelling = OK
- Content reflecting creator's unique ideas = OK even if AI-generated
- Using AI for editing, effects, music = OK

### What's NOT Allowed (Monetization Risk)
- Mass-produced AI content with minimal human input
- Same voiceover + slideshow format across videos (StoriezTold case — flagged)
- Duplicated footage, robotic cadence, repeated templates
- AI as the **entire** creative process without human direction

### Detection Methods
- Machine learning detects: duplicated footage, robotic cadence, repeated templates
- Manual reviewers investigate flagged channels
- Strike system for non-disclosure or repeat offenses
- YouTube evaluates Shorts at scale — pattern detection across channel, not per-video

### Compliance Strategy for REGGY
- Each video must have **unique visual narrative** (not just different colors in same template)
- Vary formats: paint mixing, color puzzles, transformations, different visual styles
- Human creative direction in prompt design = "AI as tool"
- Avoid: same structure/timing/layout across all videos
- Monitor for flags, diversify content types proactively
